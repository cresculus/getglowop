01 The actual problem

The calendar is making purchasing decisions.

Mid-market companies, and the MSPs who run their fleets, replace endpoints because a policy said three or four years. The carbon already happened at the factory. A newer laptop being slightly more efficient rarely pays that factory back.

A — MANUFACTURING

Manufacturing is the footprint

Germany’s environment agency, with Öko-Institut and Fraunhofer IZM, found production at about 56% of a notebook’s greenhouse gases over five years. In their scenarios, a new notebook that was only 10% more efficient took 33–89 years to “earn back” the carbon of being built.

You will not keep a laptop for 89 years. You also should not replace a healthy one to feel efficient.

B — REUSE

Reuse is the climate action

André and colleagues (Waste Management, 2019) studied a real commercial laptop-reuse operation. Getting a used laptop ready to sell again was almost nothing, environmentally, next to the carbon already in the parts. Extending use was the benefit.

ADEME’s 2022 refurbishment work, as summarized by Fraunhofer IZM, put a refurbished laptop’s footprint far below a new one.

Reuse is the climate action. Recycling is the funeral.

C — THE DATA

The data is already in the MSP’s tenant

Intune knows the model, the age, the last check-in, compliance, and — with Advanced Analytics — battery health. It will not tell a client whether that device should be kept.

Microsoft’s own sustainability tooling is strong on power policy and, for Surface, on a model emissions estimate. A mixed Dell, HP, and Lenovo fleet still gets a calendar.

D — GHOSTS

Ghost hardware is a security problem that also buys laptops

IT asset firms citing Gartner put the share of hardware that is lost, missing, or still on the books while nobody can find it around ≈30%. A device you cannot see gets replaced, and the old one is still out there with a disk.

The Global E-waste Monitor 2024 is the backdrop: 62 million tonnes of e-waste in 2022, 22.3% formally collected and recycled. Your fleet is the part an MSP can actually touch.

02 The product

The GLOW Op Report.

Businesses do not need another maturity model. They need a report they can hand a CFO, a client, or an auditor without inventing a carbon number. Some lines in that report are public. The call — keep, repair, redeploy, refresh, recycle — is ours.

Public — we cite it

We do not pretend we invented it.

  • Manufacturer product carbon footprints, including the open Boavizta compilation of those sheets. Boavizta’s own warning applies: methodologies differ, margins of error are large — use them as orders of magnitude and for same-brand comparisons.
  • The customer’s Intune / Graph inventory: make, model, serial, OS, last check-in, compliance, warranty when the tenant has it.
  • CSV upload when Graph is not available yet. The fleet still gets a report.
Proprietary — this is the GLOW Op

The judgment layer is the company.

  • GLOW Score. One number from embodied carbon, age, health, warranty, compliance, and how long the device has been quiet. The weights are ours.
  • The call, with a sentence. Keep and optimize, repair, redeploy, refresh, or certified recycle / remarket. Written for an IT manager, not a life-cycle assessor.
  • Match confidence. Exact model sheet, same-family estimate, or fallback. A guess is labeled a guess.
  • Calendar versus carbon. What the current refresh policy would buy, versus what the score says — in kilograms and in purchase dollars.
  • Persona window. A kiosk, a clinician, and a finance laptop do not share a lifespan. The mapping from the customer’s roles to a keep-window is ours.
  • Ghost flag. Stale check-in, still assigned, still on the books. Their signal. Our rule for when it shows up in the report.
  • Repair-before-replace. Battery or disk cost, supplied by the MSP, set next to the avoided purchase. We do not publish their vendor price list.
  • The client page. White-label PDF and CSV an MSP can put their name on. Scope 3 language that stays inside what the factors support. Not a full GHG Protocol inventory. Not the user’s files. We are not reading anyone’s email to save the climate.

04 How a real report gets made

Intune in. A decision out.

No new silo, no power-spy agent. Four steps, in order, with the honest limits printed on each one.

  1. 1

    Ingest →

    Microsoft Graph and Intune first, because that is the system MSPs already run. CSV if the tenant is not ready. Nerdio and other RMM exports are later — not a fantasy integration on day one.

  2. 2

    Enrich →

    Match the model to a manufacturer PCF or to Boavizta’s open compilation. If the exact sheet does not exist, say so and use a documented fallback. Do not average the internet and call it science.

  3. 3

    Decide →

    Rules first. Machine learning only after the rules have been wrong in public often enough to deserve it. Security and a dead motherboard still win. Carbon does not ask you to keep a machine that cannot take the current OS.

  4. 4

    Report ✓

    One GLOW Op Report per client. Portfolio view for the MSP. PDF and CSV. The MSP’s logo, if they want their name on the joke.

What the public code already does — and where it stops

Boavizta’s environmental-footprint-data repository is the best open pile of manufacturer PCFs; their API looks up a model. The Green Software Foundation’s SCI spec is about software, not a laptop fleet. Intune can push a power policy and, on Surface, estimate emissions. Warranty-and-refresh packs for MSPs plan a calendar. None of that writes the keep / repair / redeploy / refresh / recycle page. That gap is the company.

Out of scope — Phase 1

Phase 1 does not pick up the truck, install a power agent on every endpoint, score the data center, or replace Watershed. Physical ITAD is a partner. The report decides. Someone else with a certification hauls the dead ones.
